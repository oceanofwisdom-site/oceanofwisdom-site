02 · NUMBER THEORY

Aryabhata’s Kuttaka — Linear Congruence Solver

Solve a × x ≡ b (mod m). The Euclidean algorithm checks solvability and returns all distinct residue solutions.

Example: 26x ≡ 1 (mod 7)

This is an exact modern solver for linear congruences related to the mathematical setting of kuṭṭaka (“pulveriser”). It does not claim to reproduce every historical worked procedure verbatim.

03 · GEOMETRY

Śulbasūtra Geometry Laboratory

Enter the perpendicular sides of a right triangle to calculate its diagonal, area and perimeter. This illustrates a geometric problem relevant to altar construction.

Example: base 3, height 4

The diagonal uses the Pythagorean relation. This modern calculator does not claim that all Śulbasūtra constructions used this interface or notation.

04 · WATER & ENVIRONMENT

Rainwater Harvesting & Storage Model

Estimate runoff from rainfall, catchment area and runoff coefficient; compare the harvest with tank capacity and household demand.

Example values loaded. Select Estimate harvest.

Formula: litres = rainfall (mm) × area (m²) × runoff coefficient. It does not separately model first-flush loss, evaporation, leaks or seasonal timing. Educational estimate only, not a structural or drinking-water safety design.

Historical context and references

The Śulbasūtras preserve geometric procedures; Aryabhata’s Āryabhaṭīya contains mathematical methods including work on indeterminate problems. Water systems were adapted to local climate and terrain. This page links selected historical ideas to transparent modern calculations; it does not claim one uniform ancient Indian technology.

  • Aryabhata, Āryabhaṭīya — primary historical text; consult scholarly editions.
  • Baudhāyana and Āpastamba Śulbasūtra traditions — textual procedures and modern interpretations vary.
  • Rainwater formula here is a modern dimensional model, not an attributed ancient formula.
