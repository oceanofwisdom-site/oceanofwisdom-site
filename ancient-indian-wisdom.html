Kautilya's Arthashastra: statecraft and public administration

The Arthaśāstra is a major Sanskrit work on political economy and governance. It is traditionally associated with Kauṭilya, also known as Chanakya or Vishnugupta, and linked in later tradition with Chandragupta Maurya. Its composition is complex: scholars debate its layers, redaction and date, so it should not be presented as a securely authored single-volume manual written at one moment.

1. The state and its institutions

The text analyses the ruler, ministers, territory, fortified centres, treasury, armed force, allies and the population as interdependent elements of political power. It treats administration as a system requiring trained officials, oversight, records and accountability mechanisms.

2. Revenue, taxation and public finance

It discusses sources of state revenue, assessment, collection, expenditure, treasury management and the risks of corruption. Its prescriptions reveal an interest in predictable revenue and administrative supervision, though they should not be mistaken for a modern fiscal constitution.

3. Agriculture, irrigation and economic production

Agriculture, cultivation, water management, forests, mines, workshops, trade routes and markets appear as matters of state concern. The work considers how production and infrastructure support both livelihoods and the fiscal capacity of the state.

4. Trade, weights, measures and regulation

It addresses market supervision, prices, quality, commercial conduct, transport and measures. These passages provide evidence for normative ideas about economic regulation, not a complete statistical description of every Mauryan market.

5. Law, courts and social order

Legal procedure, evidence, contracts, property, debts, penalties and disputes are treated within a wider framework of royal authority and social norms. The text reflects its own historical setting and should not be read as a timeless or universally applicable code.

6. Diplomacy, alliances and foreign policy

The mandala model examines neighbouring rulers, potential allies, adversaries and shifting interests. It presents diplomacy as strategic assessment rather than a simple division between permanent friends and enemies.

7. Intelligence and security

Espionage and information gathering occupy a significant place. The text describes networks of agents and methods of assessing loyalty and threats. These are historical prescriptions, not ethical recommendations for contemporary governance.

8. Defence and military organisation

Fortifications, logistics, troops, commanders, terrain and preparation for conflict are considered as elements of state security. Military power is presented alongside diplomacy, finance and internal stability.

9. Reading the text critically

The Arthashastra is a prescriptive work: it describes what an idealised state should do, not necessarily what every historical ruler actually did. Its layers, terminology and transmission require specialist study. Claims connecting every passage directly to Chandragupta's administration must therefore be treated cautiously.

Research approach

Each study should identify its primary textual or material evidence, explain dating and attribution issues, distinguish reconstruction from direct evidence, and cite accessible scholarly or institutional references. Modern calculations are labelled as modern models rather than passed off as exact replicas of ancient procedures.
