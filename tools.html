Working Tools of the Ancient World

Not descriptions of old techniques — working models. Every tool here computes live, in your browser, from the numbers you enter. Nothing is sent anywhere, and they keep working with no internet connection.

How these work

Each calculator is an original, transparent implementation of a historical mathematical idea — it does not claim to reproduce every ancient worked procedure verbatim, and it never confuses the historical setting with the modern formula it uses. The mathematics is exact; the history is handled honestly.

Ancient India

Number theory

Aryabhata's Kuttaka

Solve the linear congruence a·x ≡ b (mod m). The extended Euclidean algorithm checks solvability and returns every distinct residue solution.

Enter a, b and m, then Solve.

The kuṭṭaka ("pulveriser") is Aryabhata's method for indeterminate equations of this kind.

Geometry

Śulbasūtra Geometry

Enter the two perpendicular sides of a right triangle to get its diagonal, area and perimeter — the geometry behind Vedic altar construction.

Enter base and height.

The diagonal uses the Pythagorean relation, stated in the Śulbasūtras for building fire-altars.

Water engineering

Rainwater Harvesting

Estimate runoff from rainfall, catchment area and runoff coefficient, and compare it with tank capacity and household demand.

Enter the values above.

Harvest = rainfall × area × coefficient. An educational estimate; it ignores first-flush loss, evaporation and leaks.

The wider ancient world

Mesopotamia

Babylonian Base-60

Convert a whole number to and from the Babylonian sexagesimal (base-60) place-value system that still shapes our hours and degrees.

Enter a decimal number, or base-60 digits.

Base-60 is why an hour has 60 minutes and a circle 360 degrees.

Mesoamerica

Maya Long Count

Convert a Gregorian date to the Maya Long Count (baktun.katun.tun.winal.kin), using the standard GMT correlation.

Pick a date, or enter a Long Count.

GMT correlation: Long Count day 0 = Julian Day 584283.

Calendars

Julian ↔ Gregorian

See how a date shifts between the Julian and Gregorian calendars — the gap that grew to days over the centuries.

Enter a date and its calendar.

Both calendars are converted through the Julian Day Number.

Greek mechanics

Archimedes' Lever

"Give me a place to stand and I will move the Earth." Balance a load with an effort using the law of the lever.

Enter effort and the two arm lengths.

Law of the lever: effort × effort-arm = load × load-arm.
