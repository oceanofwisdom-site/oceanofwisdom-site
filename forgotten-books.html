Forgotten Books of the Ancient World

A reading atlas of lesser-known works preserving ideas about science, architecture, medicine, language, nature and society.

This is a discovery list, not a ranking of the greatest books. Dates and authorship can be uncertain; many texts evolved through redaction, commentary and copying.

How to read an old text

Manuscript→Dating→Authorship→Genre→Critical reading
FIELD STUDY 01

Samarāṅgaṇasūtradhāra — architecture and mechanical devices

Traditionally associated with 11th-century king Bhoja, this Sanskrit treatise discusses architecture, planning, measurements, images and mechanical contrivances (yantras).

Evidence & limits: Authorship and dating should be stated cautiously; technical passages require specialist commentary.
FIELD STUDY 02

Mayamata — architecture and settlement planning

A Sanskrit Vāstuśāstra treatise on site selection, measurement, building types, temples, houses and settlement design.

Evidence & limits: It is prescriptive: it records norms and ideals, not automatically what every builder did.
FIELD STUDY 03

Aparājitapṛcchā — temples and images

A medieval Sanskrit work associated with western Indian architectural traditions, discussing temple forms, plans, measurements and iconography.

Evidence & limits: Its date, authorship and manuscript history should be checked against specialist editions.
FIELD STUDY 04

Yuktibhāṣā — reasoning behind mathematics

Composed in Malayalam by Jyeṣṭhadeva in the Kerala mathematical tradition, generally in the 16th century, it presents mathematical reasoning and proofs, including work related to infinite series and trigonometry.

Evidence & limits: Do not equate it wholesale with modern calculus; compare specific arguments and techniques.
FIELD STUDY 05

Bower Manuscript — medicine and practical knowledge

A collection of Sanskrit medical and related texts written on birch bark, discovered in Central Asia in the late 19th century and generally dated to around the 5th–6th centuries CE.

Evidence & limits: It is a manuscript collection, not one book by one author.
FIELD STUDY 06

Bṛhat Saṃhitā — an encyclopaedia of the world

Varāhamihira’s 6th-century Sanskrit compendium ranges across astronomy, calendrics, weather signs, architecture, water-finding, plants, gems and social customs.

Evidence & limits: It includes historical beliefs that should not all be treated as modern scientific findings.
FIELD STUDY 07

Baudhāyana Śulbasūtra — geometry for ritual construction

A Śulbasūtra text preserving geometric procedures for constructing ritual altars, including relations among squares, diagonals and areas.

Evidence & limits: Exact dates are debated; it belongs to a wider textual tradition, not a modern textbook.
FIELD STUDY 08

Hastividyārṇava — elephants and courtly knowledge

An illustrated Assamese court manuscript from the Ahom period recording knowledge about elephants, their care, classification and royal use.

Evidence & limits: It is early modern rather than ancient in the strict sense, included for its value to the history of knowledge.
FIELD STUDY 09

Mānasollāsa — the encyclopaedia of a king

A 12th-century Sanskrit compendium attributed to Someśvara III, covering governance, arts, music, food, sport, hunting and courtly life.

Evidence & limits: It reflects a royal-court perspective, not everyday life for all social groups.
FIELD STUDY 10

Tantrasaṅgraha — mathematical astronomy in Kerala

Nilakantha Somayaji’s 1501 work presents sophisticated mathematical astronomy, including models for planetary motion.

Evidence & limits: Read it alongside scholarly translations and histories of Indian astronomy.
