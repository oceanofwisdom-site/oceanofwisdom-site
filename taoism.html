Taoism — Laozi and the Tao

Origins and historical setting

Taoism developed in China through philosophical, religious and ritual traditions over many centuries. Laozi is traditionally associated with the Tao Te Ching, although scholars debate its authorship and composition. Taoism includes diverse schools and practices.

Central teachings

Tao is often translated as the Way. Taoist thought values simplicity, humility, balance and attentiveness to nature. Wu wei, or non-forcing action, suggests responding to circumstances without imposing rigid control.

Texts, practices and communities

The Tao Te Ching and Zhuangzi are key classical texts. Religious Taoist traditions also developed temples, liturgies, meditation, ethical disciplines and healing practices. Yin and yang describe complementary dynamics, not simply good and evil.

Legacy and continuing life

Taoist ideas influenced Chinese poetry, painting, medicine, landscape design and approaches to personal cultivation. Taoism interacted with Confucianism and Buddhism through debate and shared cultural settings.
