World Literature & Classical Texts

Original introductions to literary, religious, philosophical and scientific works from civilisations around the world, excluding the Indian literary collection presented separately.

Indian Classic Wisdom and Literature → now brings the Indian literary traditions together on a dedicated page. This World Literature collection focuses on West Asia, Africa, Europe, East Asia and the Americas. It distinguishes traditional accounts, historical evidence and modern interpretation.

West Asia and North Africa

Epic of Gilgamesh

Mesopotamian epic poetry exploring kingship, friendship, mortality and the search for enduring life. Surviving versions preserve a long history of composition and transmission.

Ancient Egyptian literature

Funerary compositions, wisdom instructions, hymns, narratives and administrative texts illuminate Egyptian religious and social worlds.

Hebrew Bible / Tanakh

A diverse collection of law, narrative, poetry, prophecy and wisdom literature, preserved and interpreted across Jewish traditions.

Bible and early Christian literature

Canons, languages, translation histories and interpretive traditions differ among Christian communities.

Bible Odyssey — scholarly introductions →

Qur'an

Islam's central scripture, studied through language, recitation, structure, interpretation and historical transmission.

Quran.com — text and translations →

Avesta

The Zoroastrian textual corpus includes the Gathas and other liturgical and religious compositions preserved through oral and manuscript traditions.

Greek and Roman classics

Homeric epics

The Iliad and Odyssey explore war, honour, homecoming, divine agency and human vulnerability in ancient Greek epic poetry.

Greek philosophy

Works associated with Plato, Aristotle and other thinkers shaped debates about knowledge, ethics, politics, nature and metaphysics.

Greek drama and history

Tragedy, comedy and historical writing developed distinctive approaches to civic life, conflict, memory and responsibility.

Roman literature

Virgil, Ovid, Cicero, Seneca and others contributed epic, poetry, rhetoric, philosophy, history and political writing.

East Asian traditions

Chinese classics

The Analects, Dao De Jing, Zhuangzi and other works shaped Confucian, Daoist and wider Chinese intellectual traditions.

Japanese literary classics

The Tale of Genji, poetry anthologies, court diaries and Buddhist writing reveal changing Japanese aesthetic and social worlds.

Korean literary traditions

Classical poetry, historical chronicles, Buddhist texts and vernacular literature reflect Korea's distinctive literary history.

African and Indigenous American traditions

African oral and written traditions

Epic cycles, praise poetry, proverbs, chronicles, religious writing and modern literature carry knowledge through diverse languages and communities.

Maya and Mesoamerican texts

The Popol Vuh, inscriptions and codices provide windows into creation narratives, dynastic history, ritual and calendrical knowledge.

Andean and Indigenous traditions

Oral histories, material records, visual systems and colonial-era transcriptions preserve diverse intellectual traditions that must be approached with attention to community context.

Free-to-read primary texts and digital libraries

Use a digital collection as a starting point, then check the edition, translator, date and rights statement for the particular text. “Free to read” does not always mean “free to republish.”

  • Oregon State University — Ancient Civilizations: a free primary-source reader
  • University of Oxford — Electronic Text Corpus of Sumerian Literature
  • Tufts University — Perseus Digital Library: Greek and Roman texts, tools and artefacts
  • Chinese Text Project — pre-modern Chinese texts and translations
  • Project Gutenberg — public-domain books and translations
  • MIT Libraries — digitised early Arabic sources for Islamic history
  • VedaWeb — open research environment for Vedic texts
