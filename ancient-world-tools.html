Editorial and technical standard
These original educational calculators compute from your inputs. Calendar conventions, historical correlations and idealised physical assumptions are displayed rather than hidden. They do not claim to recreate every ancient procedure.
01 · MESOPOTAMIAN MATHEMATICS

Babylonian Base-60 Mathematics Laboratory

Convert a non-negative decimal number into sexagesimal place values, or enter base-60 digits to convert back to decimal. This is positional arithmetic, not a claim about a specific Babylonian tablet algorithm.

3661.5 = 1;1;1;30

Digits are separated by semicolons. This converter uses modern positional notation; historical Babylonian notation and treatment of empty places varied by period.

02 · CALENDRICAL HISTORY

Julian ↔ Gregorian Date Converter

Convert a civil date between the proleptic Julian and Gregorian calendars. The calendar system is selected explicitly; this tool does not assume the local historical adoption date.

15 October 1582 Julian → 25 October 1582 Gregorian

Uses astronomical year numbering, including year 0. Output is the corresponding date in the other proleptic calendar. Historical civil calendars changed at different dates in different regions.

03 · MAYA CALENDRICAL SYSTEM

Maya Long Count Converter

Convert Long Count components to elapsed days and an associated Gregorian date using a selectable Goodman–Martínez–Thompson (GMT) correlation constant.

13.0.0.0.0 · GMT 584283 → 21 December 2012 Gregorian

The standard Long Count place values are 1, 20, 360, 7,200 and 144,000 days. The correlation constant is debated; change it to explore alternatives. The displayed Gregorian date uses the proleptic Gregorian calendar.

04 · CLASSICAL MECHANICS

Archimedean Lever & Balance Calculator

For an ideal lever in static balance, calculate the unknown load or effort from forces and distances to the fulcrum. Compare torque on both sides.

100 N × 0.5 m balances 25 N × 2 m

Ideal static model: force₁ × arm₁ = force₂ × arm₂. It ignores friction, lever weight, deformation and dynamic effects. It is an educational mechanics calculator, not a lifting safety tool.

Historical context and method

Mesopotamian scribes used sexagesimal place-value mathematics in astronomy, metrology and administration. Julian and Gregorian calendars are distinct rule systems whose civil adoption histories varied by region. The Maya Long Count records elapsed days using a modified vigesimal structure. The lever law is expressed here through modern statics, while Archimedes’ historical work is treated as context rather than a claim that this page reproduces a lost ancient machine.

  • Babylonian mathematical tablets and sexagesimal metrology: consult museum catalogues and scholarly editions.
  • Calendar algorithms: Julian and Gregorian leap-year rules are explicitly separated in the converter.
  • Maya Long Count: GMT correlation 584283 is a widely used convention, not the only proposal.
  • Lever model: torque equilibrium in ideal statics; no real-world load certification.
