Texts & manuscripts

How to read an ancient text

An ancient text is not simply a window into the past. It is a work shaped by language, genre, audience, memory and the circumstances of its preservation. A hymn, legal inscription, epic poem and philosophical dialogue each make different kinds of claims and must be read differently.

Begin by asking who composed or commissioned it, when and where it took shape, which manuscript or inscription survives, and whether the text has passed through layers of editing or translation. Compare reliable editions and translations, distinguish the words of the source from later commentary, and look for independent archaeological or documentary evidence. Where dates or authorship remain disputed, state that uncertainty rather than forcing a single answer.

Astronomy & science

Indian mathematical astronomy: observation and calculation

South Asian astronomers developed sophisticated procedures for calendrical calculation, predicting celestial positions and describing planetary motion. Works associated with Aryabhata, Varahamihira and later astronomers belong to a changing intellectual history, not one unaltered system.

These texts combine mathematical models, parameters, tables and observational traditions. Their historical importance lies in how scholars formulated problems, refined calculations and transmitted methods. A responsible comparison with modern astronomy separates historical achievement from present-day scientific accuracy: an old computational model can be intellectually remarkable without being a current physical description of the heavens.

Earth & ecology

Traditional water harvesting: tanks, stepwells and community systems

Across the Indian subcontinent, communities developed diverse ways to collect, store and distribute seasonal water. Tanks, stepwells, village ponds, channels, wells and rooftop collection systems responded to local rainfall, geology, settlement patterns and agricultural needs.

These systems were not interchangeable, nor were they always sustainable in every period. Their performance depended on maintenance, access rules, catchment protection and collective institutions. Studying them today means examining local engineering and social organisation together, then adapting useful principles—such as slowing runoff, replenishing groundwater and maintaining shared infrastructure—to contemporary conditions.

Civilizations & history

What archaeology can—and cannot—tell us

Archaeology reconstructs past lives through material evidence: buildings, tools, pottery, food remains, burials, inscriptions and the layers in which objects are found. Context is crucial. An object without a secure findspot or date may tell us far less than a modest artefact recovered from a carefully recorded excavation.

Material evidence can reveal technologies, trade, diet, settlement patterns and environmental change. It rarely provides direct access to every belief, language or political event. Researchers therefore compare archaeological findings with texts, scientific analyses and regional evidence, while keeping competing interpretations visible. The absence of evidence is not automatically evidence of absence.

Philosophy & traditions

Selfhood and liberation in Indian philosophy

Indian philosophical traditions ask whether the self is permanent, changing, relational or ultimately misunderstood. Upanishadic schools, Buddhist thinkers and Jain philosophers developed distinct answers, using careful argument as well as contemplative and ethical disciplines.

Some Vedanta traditions identify the deepest self with ultimate reality; Buddhist analyses challenge the idea of an independent, enduring self; Jain thought describes living beings as individual conscious substances. These positions should not be collapsed into a single “Eastern view.” Their differences are precisely what make comparison fruitful: each offers a distinctive account of knowledge, responsibility and freedom.

Arts & culture

Temple architecture as a record of society

Temples are religious spaces, but they are also historical records. Their plans, construction techniques, sculpture, inscriptions and patronage reveal changing relationships among artisans, rulers, donors, ritual specialists and local communities.

Architecture varies by region and period. A careful study identifies the building's date, materials, later additions and original setting before assigning it to a style or dynasty. Inscriptions and archaeological context can illuminate how a monument was used and maintained. This approach lets us appreciate artistic achievement while avoiding the assumption that every temple expresses one uniform belief or political purpose.
